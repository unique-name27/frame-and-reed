    How the fit works

    Every case starts from the harp's outline. The pocket is that outline grown by the clearance you set, the hood rises only around the trigger, and the deck sits 1.5 mm over the frame so the case stays as thin as the harp allows.

    A photo works best shot straight down on plain paper with the harp flat and nothing else in frame; the outline is then scaled by the length you type, so measure that once with a ruler. Two things a photo cannot see, so type them: the harp's height lying flat, underside to the tip of the trigger, and the thickness of its frame. Stock sizes follow the real families: the round forged bow of European harps and the morchang, the flat stamped oval of Hohner-pattern frames, and the long loop of the Yakut khomus and bass harps. For anything else, photograph the harp from above on graph paper and trace one side — harps are symmetrical, so the other side is mirrored.

    captive turn-buttons, Ø4 peg, Ø6 head in a chamber, sweep recessed
    Hood roof
    3 mm, over the trigger end; the harp slides back out from under it
    Sleeve gate
    captive turn-button at the mouth
    Lid latch
    5 × 3 mm sliding bolt in a roofed channel, 6 mm throw into a keeper tunnel
    Hinge
    5 knuckles, Ø6.4 on a shelf, Ø2 bore, 1.75 mm filament pin
    Bail
    Ø6.5 hole, 5 mm rim

    Printing it yourself

    Slice each STL as it comes, flat on the bed, no supports, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers — at that height the 0.4 mm print gap is exactly two layers of air above and below every moving part. The file is a set of closed, overlapping solids; leave your slicer's union of overlapping shells on (it is the default in PrusaSlicer, Bambu Studio and Cura). Every moving part is separated from the body by the print gap — 0.4 mm is right for most printers in PLA; PETG likes to weld across small gaps, so print the first one in PLA or set the gap to 0.5. After printing, a firm quarter-turn breaks each button free; it then clicks into its locked position on a small detent. The clamshell comes as base and lid, both printed flat; a length of 1.75 mm filament pushed through the knuckles and trimmed flush makes the hinge pin.

    A design code carries every setting, including a traced outline, so someone else can load it here and print the same case. It is also written into the README in your zip.
