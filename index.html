    About the fit

    It all comes from the outline of your harp. The pocket is that outline plus whatever clearance you set, the hood only rises where the trigger is, and the deck sits over the frame, so the case is no thicker than it has to be.

    Photos work best on plain paper, harp lying flat, nothing else in the shot. The outline gets scaled by the length you type in, so measure that one dimension properly and the rest follows. Two things a photo cannot see, so you type those as well: how tall the harp is lying flat, underside to the tip of the trigger, and how thick the frame is. The stock sizes follow the real families, so a forged European bow, a stamped Hohner-pattern frame or a Yakut khomus will land close. If yours is none of them, trace one side by hand and the other side mirrors.

    Printing it

    Slice it as it comes: flat on the bed, no supports, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers. At that layer height the 0.4 mm print gap works out to exactly two layers of air above and below anything that moves. Leave the slicer's union of overlapping shells switched on. It is the default in PrusaSlicer, Bambu Studio and Cura, and the file counts on it.

    Print the first one in PLA. PETG likes to weld itself across small gaps and you can end up with a latch that never frees; if you want PETG anyway, set the print gap to first. A latch that will not shift usually just needs a firmer push, since the first movement has to break the print skin.

    The clamshell prints as a base and a lid, and a short piece of 1.75 mm filament through the hinge knuckles, snipped flush, is the pin. The sliding cover prints flat beside the case and slides in afterwards. Cord lashing needs about of cord, or shock cord if you would rather it snapped back.
